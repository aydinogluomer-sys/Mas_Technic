### D-09b2-01 — `ScrollToTop.tsx:53` bounds the landing by a frame count, and the count expires on a slow load (medium, **blocks acceptance criterion 5 and 9**)

`HASH_SETTLE_FRAMES = 90` (~1.5 s at 60 Hz) polls for the clause element and then calls `toTop()`.
On a full navigation the clause exists only after the `lazy()` route chunk mounts, so the fix holds
exactly when that happens inside 90 frames. Measured with a fresh context per row
(`09b1r2-fragment-cold.txt`):

```
run  profile        element appeared at   final clause top   verdict
1 cold                511 ms            0 px             landed
2 warm                557 ms            0 px             landed
3 warm                486 ms            0 px             landed
4 warm 4x CPU        2905 ms         2606 px             *** AT THE TOP — the budget expired first ***
5 warm 6x CPU        6290 ms         2606 px             *** AT THE TOP ***
```

At **4× CPU** — a mid-range phone, not an absurd profile — the reader who pastes the URL lands at the
top of a seven-clause document, which is the defect this item exists to fix, reproduced by the fix's
own timeout. The Playwright red is the same mechanism: the first load in a fresh worker took long
enough (12.7 s test duration against 2.9 s warm) to exceed the budget.

This is the same shape as `RETURN_MAX_AGE_MS`: a clock standing in for a condition. The condition
is "the route has mounted"; the poll should give up when the route has rendered **and** the id is
still absent (e.g. `.shell-boot` gone and the target still null), not after N frames. The give-up
branch to `toTop()` is otherwise right — a hash naming nothing must not strand the reader at the
previous offset.

The spec is correct and should stay exactly as written: it is the instrument that caught this.
